# Ana — what actually performs, and the idea bank that follows from it

Last updated 2026-07-27.

## The three highest-engagement Ana posts so far

1. **Anthropic hiring field marketers** (2026-05-30) — newsjack with a screenshot.
2. **One month since Demo Day / what I liked about the YC batch** (2026-07-16) — insider list.
3. **Cinquenta anos em cinco** (2026-07-18) — Brazilian history mapped onto YC.

## What they have in common

**None of them are about the product.** Two mention Pavoot in a single line, one not at all.

Each brings the feed something it cannot get elsewhere and then translates it:
- Anthropic's job board — news the reader had not connected to anything.
- The inside of YC — a room the reader cannot enter.
- Brazilian history — knowledge the reader does not have.

So Ana's edge is **access, background, and news literacy**, not vision takes and not product
demos. The vision posts are useful for positioning and for pairing with Gohar, but they are not
what drives reach on her profile.

Secondary drivers visible in all three: **identity sharing** (Brazilians share the Brazil posts,
YC alumni share the YC posts), **debate** (the Anthropic post invites disagreement), and
**saveability** (the numbered list).

**Rule of thumb for Ana's next post:** if it could have been written by any other event-tech
founder, it will underperform. If it required being Ana to write it, it will do well.

## Lane A — borrowed attention (the Anthropic formula)

Big external peg + a screenshot as proof + a reframe that celebrates event marketers (our buyer,
never the target). Requires real, verifiable facts. Ana supplies the numbers, never invent them.

- **The job board sweep.** Go through the careers pages of the ten most valuable AI companies and
  count how many are hiring field or event marketers. Screenshot the grid.
  *"I went through the careers pages of the 10 most valuable AI companies in the world. [N] of them
  are hiring event marketers right now."* Direct sequel to the biggest hit, with a harder claim.
  NEEDS: the actual count.
- **What one flagship conference costs.** Dreamforce, re:Invent, or similar, with a public number.
  *"[Company] spends more on one conference than most startups raise in a Series A. And it is the
  best money they spend."* NEEDS: a sourced figure.
- **The incumbent's age.** Cvent founded 1999, still ships badge-scanner hardware. Screenshot the
  hardware page. *"The software running your conference was built before the iPhone."*
- **Live AI news peg.** Reusable slot. Any major model release, raise, or AI-replaces-jobs cycle
  can be turned into "and yet the smartest companies keep investing in the room." Needs a fresh
  peg on the day, so this one gets written when the news lands, not before.

## Lane B — the closed room (the YC formula)

Take people somewhere they cannot go. Be specific. Number it when it fits.

- **The office hours you kept.** The 7/16 post ended with "the next one is next week." Report back.
  *"We kept doing YC group office hours after the batch ended. [N] weeks later, here is what
  changed."* Sequels to hits perform, and this one is already set up.
- **What actually happens after Demo Day.** Everyone posts the pitch. Nobody posts the weeks after,
  when the structure disappears and you are just a company again.
- **What investors actually ask.** The three questions that came up in every conversation, and the
  one you were worst at answering. Check disclosure comfort on round status first.
- **How the pitch changed.** Three specific things you cut, and why. High save rate.
- **What YC costs you.** The honest, slightly contrarian version. Only if there is a real answer.

## Lane C — Brazil (the fifty-years formula)

An untranslatable word or a piece of history the feed has never heard, mapped onto a universal
startup truth. Verify historical specifics before publishing, the comments will contain Brazilians.

- **Gambiarra.** ✅ WRITTEN — `posts/2026-07-27-gambiarra.md`
- **Semana de Arte Moderna, 1922.** Three nights at the Teatro Municipal in São Paulo. Mário and
  Oswald de Andrade, Anita Malfatti, Villa-Lobos. The audience booed and walked out. It went on to
  define a century of Brazilian culture. **Angle:** attendance is a vanity metric, the thing that
  mattered was who was in the seats and nobody could have measured it that night.
  *"The most important event in Brazilian history was, by every metric available that night, a
  failure."* Strongest remaining idea in this lane.
- **Rock in Rio, 1985.** Roberto Medina, an ad executive, wanted to bring Queen to Brazil. No venue
  in the country could hold it, so he built one from empty ground in Jacarepaguá. Over a million
  people across ten days, and it happened just as Brazil was coming out of the dictatorship. It is
  now a global franchise. **Angle:** the infrastructure did not exist, so he built the
  infrastructure. That is the founder move.
  *"In 1984 a Brazilian ad guy wanted to book Queen. There was no venue in the country big enough,
  so he built a city."*
- **Escola de samba.** A full year of work by hundreds of people in a warehouse to produce roughly
  seventy minutes on the Sapucaí, judged on fixed criteria, penalised for going over time.
  **Angle:** the most disciplined event operation on earth, and it has been one-shot-no-rerun for a
  century. Works in Gohar's voice too. VERIFY the time limit and judging criteria.
- **Eco-92.** Over a hundred heads of state in Rio, June 1992. Produced the framework that became
  Kyoto and then Paris. **Angle:** the Paris Agreement was decided in Rio thirty years earlier.
  Weakest of the set, it is the most conventional "events matter" argument and it is political.
- **Bossa nova at Carnegie Hall, 1962.** João Gilberto, Jobim, Sérgio Mendes. By most accounts
  badly produced. It still detonated bossa nova in the US, because the audience was full of
  American jazz musicians and record executives. **Angle:** the production was bad and the outcome
  was historic, because the guest list was right.
- **Jeitinho brasileiro.** Adjacent to gambiarra but riskier, it also means bending the rules.
  That ambiguity is either the interesting part or the problem, depending on how honestly it is
  written. Hold until there is a real story to hang it on.

## Added 2026-08-14 (after the Lovable careers-page post)

The careers-page move is now a **series**, not a one-off. Anthropic (7/13) and Lovable (8/14) both
worked off the same shape: big raise or big news lands → open the careers page → count the GTM
roles → reframe around the buyer. Two data points is enough to name it. Keep running it on every
major AI raise and let readers start expecting it.

**Ready to write today, no fact needed from Ana:**
- **Cvent vs Lovable.** Cvent was founded in 1999, does $700M+ revenue, and PE bought the whole
  company for $4.6B. Lovable is 21 months old and just priced at $13.3B. The incumbent in our
  category is worth a third of a company that did not exist two years ago.
  *"The biggest company in event software sold for $4.6B. A 21-month-old startup in Stockholm is
  worth three times that."* Sourced on both sides. This is the strongest Lane A idea in the file.
- **Semana de Arte Moderna, 1922.** Still the strongest Brazil idea. See Lane C above.
- **Rock in Rio, 1985.** See Lane C above.

**Needs one fact or screenshot from Ana:**
- **The full careers-page sweep.** The ten most valuable AI companies, how many are hiring event or
  field marketers, as a screenshot grid. The hardest version of the claim and the natural third
  entry in the series. Someone has to actually run the count.
- **The office hours sequel.** Set up by the 7/16 post. Needs N weeks and what changed.
- **One month with an engineer.** We hired in late July. What actually changed, honestly, including

## Notes for whoever drafts these

- Ana's proven structure for Lane C: **untranslated word or phrase as a one-line hook → plain
  explanation → the mapping → land on a principle, not on Pavoot.**
- Do not bolt a product pitch onto a Lane B or C post. The winners did not have one.
- Every Lane A idea needs a real number or a real screenshot from Ana. None of them can be written
  without her supplying the fact.
