## 1. What Pavoot is

- **Lead line now [PUBLIC]**: an AI event agency. "We fill the room. You meet the people." Companies say who they
  want to meet; Pavoot finds and invites the right people, books the venue and runs the event, with its own
  software doing the work in the background. (pavoot.com headline: "Rooms people want to be in.")
- **Longer version [PUBLIC]**: "an AI agent that runs company events end to end. Think of it as a forward-deployed
  event marketer that sits inside your team and owns the whole event strategy, with an event CRM underneath it.
  Every event leaves behind who came, who mattered and what it drove, so the next one is planned off that
  instead of a spreadsheet and whatever people remember."
- **Older tagline, still fine [PUBLIC]**: "AI that turns events into pipeline." Use carefully (see §6 on attribution).
- **Lines the founders actually use [PUBLIC]**: "software is getting cheaper; clients want outcomes, not tools";
  "what we won't automate: in-person relationships"; "the most expensive channel in B2B marketing is also the
  worst measured one"; buyer quote they hear: "I have the money but not the headspace."
- **Why service first [PUBLIC as a story]**: software buyers didn't adopt because setup still took work, so
  Pavoot runs the events itself: "we are better users of our software than anybody." (Aug–Oct 2026 shift.)
- **Name [PUBLIC]**: Pavo (peacock) + Argus, the giant with a thousand eyes; the logo carries Brazilian colours
  and Mount Ararat.

## 2. The three offers

### a) Service: done-for-you events (the lead offer)
- Included [PUBLIC]: the right guests (sourced and invited), invitations and RSVPs, venue and hosting, landing
  page, reminders, check-in, notes during the event, follow-ups based on what each guest said, a shared recap
  with photos.
- Formats [PUBLIC]: dinners (15–20), happy hours and mixers (50–120), panels, co-hosted events where several
  companies with the same audience share one room.
- Pricing [INTERNAL]: around $10k per event all-in (sourcing-only ~$5k; co-hosts or sponsors from ~$3k each;
  pilot then a series of 4–5). Never put prices in posts.
- "Venue and hosting" is the team's work, not software. Say "we handle", never "the AI books the venue".

### b) Software: the AI event platform (for teams that run events themselves)
Shipped and safe to describe [PUBLIC]:
- **Before**: describe your ideal guest → AI sources and scores people (CRM + open web); personalised invites
  written per person and sent from your own Gmail/Outlook; event pages and registration with approval;
  Luma and Partiful guest-list sync; paid ticketing (Stripe); pre-event briefing (who to meet, warm paths).
- **During**: notetaker app (iOS, approved Oct 2026) and web check-in: QR check-in, voice notes, scanning
  business cards, badges and LinkedIn QR codes; matchmaking intros.
- **After**: follow-ups drafted per guest from the notes; recap report with share link; attendance insights
  across events (show-up rates, returning guests); HubSpot and Salesforce sync (attendance, notes, tasks,
  campaigns); an event CRM with AI chat over every past event.
- **Photos ("Pavoot Photos")**: faces matched to the guest list, personalised galleries sent to each guest.
  Real and used at Zurich Film Festival and START Summit, but **don't lead with it** (§6).
- Also real: an MCP server so Claude or Cursor can drive Pavoot.
Not shipped, never claim: "meetings booked" tracking, automatic revenue/pipeline attribution, a run-of-show
schedule. LinkedIn outreach and SMS exist only for accounts Pavoot enables: say "on request" at most.
- Software price [INTERNAL]: ~$1k/month for ~5–8 events.

### c) pavoot.events: the marketplace (built, not launched)
- What it is [PUBLIC once live; ASK before announcing]: "Events worth being in." Guests say what brings them
  (attend, host or sponsor, speak, photograph), get personalised event matches, and can "ask for an event".
  Companies back event ideas; when enough people plus a host and a sponsor are in, Pavoot sets the date and
  curates the room, and sponsors share the cost. Photographers set their price. Pledges aren't binding.
- Status [INTERNAL]: launch has slipped several times (Oct 9: "2 weeks", Oct 20: "next week", Oct 23: "in
  development"). Matching is manual for now. **Do not announce a launch until Ana confirms it's live.**

## 4. Who we write for (ICP and their words)

- Buyer [PUBLIC]: the GTM, demand-gen or field-marketing owner (Head of Events, field marketing, Head of
  Startups/ecosystem, first GTM hire, CMO/VP Marketing) at a B2B company past product-market fit that runs, or

  companies too. Not brand-new founders without budget.
- Their words (use the ideas, never attribute the quotes) [PUBLIC]: "filling the room"; "wrong people in the
  room"; "120 registered, 60 RSVPs, 10 showed up"; "5–10 Excel sheets per event"; follow-up is "systematically
  weak"; "deals close 6–12 months later, so nobody can attribute the event"; "accepted random people because
  they were afraid the room would be empty".
- Lessons from ~100 events that make good posts [PUBLIC]: curated rooms beat open Luma listings (they attract
  low-signal RSVPs); approval and timing of replies matter; specific invite copy with a concrete reason to
  come beats generic; co-hosting with non-competing companies that share an audience fills a room cheaper.
