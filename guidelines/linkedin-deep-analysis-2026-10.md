# What Ana's LinkedIn data actually shows (deep analysis)

Source: `Complete_LinkedInDataExport_10-04-2026.zip`, joined with post metrics from `the "LinkedIn posts" Notion database (stats columns)` (impressions through Aug 18). Window: April to early October 2026.

Companion files: `(not copied: analysis options note)` (what's possible) and `what-works-for-ana.md` (post performance through Aug 18).

**How to read the numbers.** LinkedIn doesn't say who saw a post. The main proxy here: for every post, take the connection invites Ana received in the next 72 hours and look up each sender's job title. "Buyer" means a marketing, growth, events, field, sales, GTM, or partnerships title. Titles were classified with keyword rules, so expect roughly ±10% noise per bucket. Posts close together share windows, and late May is inflated by Demo Day week.

---

## The short version

1. **What a post is about barely changes who connects.** Every type of post, whether news pegs, YC journey, personal, speaking, or posts written for marketers, brings in **11–14% buyers and around 30% founders**. Topic is not the lever for audience. Distribution is: who sees the post in the first place.
2. **Being in the room beats being in the feed.** Posts tied to something Ana did in person (AWS talk, judging the YC x Medplum hackathon) had ~2.9k impressions but pulled in **133–252 invites**, about 50x more invites per impression than the Anthropic peg. The real business conversations in the DMs started at events Ana hosted or spoke at.
3. **Inbound buyer demand peaked in June and has since dried up.** All three clearly inbound buyer threads (Mintlify, Hyperbound, ION) arrived in June, each right after a traction or Demo Day post. Since August, no marketer has messaged Ana asking about the product. Inbound conversations overall fell from 121 in June to 49 in September.
4. **Posts written for marketers get the best engagement rate and the least reach.** Median 3.11% engagement (best of any category) at 3.9k median impressions. The marketers who do see them engage. Not enough of them see them.
5. **LinkedIn's algorithm has Ana filed under "startups," not "marketing."** Between Aug 7 and Sep 10, her Entrepreneurship score rose while her Campaigns/Lifecycle Marketing and Brand/PR scores fell sharply. "Event speaker" is still 0.

**What stands out:**
- **Buyer % is the same everywhere.** No post type shifts the mix. The people who see Ana's posts are her network and its neighbors, and those are founders.
- **Pegs win impressions and lose everything else.** The Anthropic post (135k impressions) brought in 38 buyers. The AWS talk post (2.9k impressions) brought in 31.
- **Speaking posts convert attention to connections ~10x better than anything else.** Caveat: some of those invites come from people who met Ana at the event itself, not from the post. That's still the point. The event plus the post together is the mechanism.
- **Posts with the most buyers in absolute terms:** Anthropic peg (38), AWS talk (31), Gohar's birthday (28, same week, overlapping), Medplum judging (12), Gambiarra (11, best non-Demo-Day week).
- **Only post with more buyers than founders:** "We spent months studying how the best B2B teams get pipeline" (8/21): 5 buyers vs 3 founders, out of 18 invites total.

**September posts underperformed on every proxy.** Baseline incoming invites on days without a post fell to 3.8/day (lowest since April), and the 9/18 sellers post brought 4 invites total. A fresh analytics export would confirm whether impressions dropped too.

**Timing (posts with metrics, small sample):** posts published 18:00–20:00 UTC (11am–1pm SF, 8–10pm Zurich) had the best median rate (2.75%) and impressions (8.7k). Tuesday had the highest median impressions. Treat this as a tiebreaker, not a rule.

## 5. Comments: where Ana shows up

485 comments total. In May, 47% were thank-yous on Ana's own posts. The rest are short (median **4 words**) and mostly congratulations to friends, Brazilian contacts, YC peers, and co-hosts.

Since August there's essentially **no commenting on marketers' or field marketers' posts**. That's untouched distribution: a substantive comment on a field marketing leader's post puts Ana in front of exactly that person's audience, which is the audience she's missing.

Follows: 314 people and 267 companies, heavily YC and founder accounts. The feed Ana reads is a founder feed, which also shapes what she engages with and what LinkedIn shows her.

## What to do with this

Ordered by expected impact on reaching buyers.

1. **Go get the buyers directly.** The May marketer-invite campaign is the biggest source of buyers in the network. Rerun it: 50–100 field marketers and event leads per week, with a short note (75% vs 51% accept rate). Track the accept rate in the next export.
2. **Use events as the content engine.** Host or speak, then post about it the same day, tagging the co-hosts and organizers. This is the highest-converting pattern in the data, and it matches the agency model.
3. **Comment where marketers are.** 10 minutes a day on posts by field marketing leaders and event organizers, with substantive comments (not 4-word congrats). It's the only lever that puts Ana in front of a different audience without changing the network first.
4. **Bring back concrete product and traction posts.** HubSpot integration, 4.5x, and Stripe pilot are what preceded every inbound buyer. September had none.
5. **Push buyer-language posts through the company page and tagged customers,** not only Ana's profile. Her profile's audience is fixed at ~12% buyers whatever she writes about.
6. **Make speaking legible to LinkedIn.** Add talks to the profile's featured section, and name the event, stage, and topic clearly in posts, to try to move "Event speaker" off 0.
7. **Follow up open items:** Deel co-host sponsorship (answer by ~Oct 6–7), dotconferences intro, Rho suggestion, and the Zurich intro Gohar picked up.

## Method notes
- Role classification uses keyword rules on titles (English and Portuguese). "Growth" counts as marketing, and "partnerships" and "account manager" as sales.
- Invite windows are 72 hours after the post timestamp. "Excess" invites subtract the month's baseline invites/day on days without a post.
- Invites from people who never accepted (or whom Ana didn't accept) have no title and are excluded from role shares. 1,956 of 2,136 incoming invites were matched to titles.
- DM categories were checked by reading the threads, not only by keywords. Several "leads" flagged by keyword turned out to be vendors and were excluded.
