# Craft: how a great Pavoot post is made (read with voice.md)

Distilled from what performed on Ana's profile, the post-outcome data, and current practice (2026). Sources at the
bottom. Where this file and `voice.md` overlap, follow the stricter rule. `learning/learnings.md` (built from real
outcomes) beats both when it has evidence.

## 1. What the platform rewards now (2026)

- **Consistency in one lane.** LinkedIn's ranking now reads the whole profile, not just the post. Posts that match
  what Ana and Gohar are known for (events, GTM, founders building in the open, ETH/YC/Brazil/chess) travel further
  than off-topic ones. Every post must connect to the lane, newsjacks included.
- **The first 1–2 lines carry the post.** Only 2–3 lines show before "see more". They must stop the scroll AND
  signal the topic.
- **Saves, dwell time and real comments beat likes.** Frameworks, checklists, numbers that feel wrong and honest
  stories get saved and read to the end. Generic opinions don't.
- **Depth of discussion beats engagement bait.** "Comment YES if…" is penalised. Ask a real question only when you
  want the answers.
- **Links:** keep external links out of the post body; put the link in the first comment (common practice; the
  exact penalty is unverified).
- **Hashtags:** 0–2, or none.
- **Cadence:** 3–5 posts a week across both founders, one per profile per day, sustained for months beats bursts.
  Whether a "golden hour" exists is disputed; what's certain is that the founders replying to comments and the
  cofounder's substantive comment early both help.
- **Formats:** document/carousel posts get the highest engagement of any format; real photos beat stock; text-only
  is fine for strong stories. Flag a carousel when a post has steps, a comparison, a named framework or 5+ items.

## 2. The funnel (plan the week with it)

| Stage | Share of posts | What it does | Pavoot examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top: attention | ~30% | reach, new followers, borrowed attention | newsjacks (Lane A), contrarian takes, founder life with access (inside YC, the room) |
| Middle: trust | ~50% | saves, buyer DMs, "these people know events" | lessons from ~100 events: show-up rates, curation, follow-up timing, co-hosting math, invite copy that works; named frameworks |
| Bottom: conversion | ≤20% (max 1 in 5) | meetings | a specific event you're running (only if confirmed), a result told as a story, "we run your next dinner" |

## 3. Pillars for Pavoot (80% of posts sit in one of these)

1. **Filling the right room**: sourcing, curation, approval, show-up rates, the wrong-people problem.
2. **Events as GTM that pays off**: follow-up, attribution honesty, co-hosting, what CMOs ask after an event.
3. **Building Pavoot in the open**: two technical founders, YC, service-first lesson, what still needs humans.
4. **Borrowed attention (Lane A)**: big news reframed for event and field marketers (see §5).
The other 20%: personal (Brazil, chess, olympiads, ETH), always with a line back to the lane.

## 4. Hooks

Strong (pick one, write 3 variants, keep the best):
- **A number that feels wrong**: "120 registered. 60 RSVPed. 10 showed up."
- **Tension / contradiction**: "We can tell in a second if someone belongs in the room. Then we wait."
- **A claim people will argue with**: "Open Luma listings are the reason your dinner felt random."
- **A scene, mid-action**: "11pm, the night before the dinner, two seats left and one CMO hasn't replied."
- **The news peg + the twist**: "Anthropic just raised $65B. The job they're hiring for most isn't engineering."
Weak (avoid): opening with a question, a generic statement, "I'm excited to…", starting with "I" unless the rest
is surprising, any opener from the banned list.
The hook must say what the post is about (topic signal), not just tease.

## 5. Newsjacking (Lane A, the Anthropic formula)

The Anthropic post (May 30) was the founders' biggest buyer magnet so far under fair attribution: big news, fast,
reframed for event marketers, with proof. Do it deliberately:
1. **Fresh**: the story broke < 24–48 h ago. Older = skip.
2. **Big**: people in tech/GTM already talk about it (top AI/tech raises, launches, layoffs and hiring sprees, a
   viral debate, a major conference like Dreamforce/Web Summit/SaaStr/Slush, an events-industry move).
3. **Honest bridge**: a true link to in-person events, field marketing, GTM or curated rooms. Tie it to Pavoot's
   promise ("the room is the channel AI can't automate"), never to a product feature.
4. **Proof**: a screenshot, job post, quote or number from a public source the reader can check.
5. **Add insight, not noise**: a counterpoint, a pattern ("3 of the 10 biggest AI companies are hiring event
   marketers"), or what it means for the reader's next event.
6. **Celebrate the buyer**: event and field marketers come out as the winners, never replaced.
7. **Risk check**: no tragedy, layoffs framed as opportunity, politics, or a story still developing in a way that
   could flip. No Bookface or private info.
Structure: peg (1–2 lines) → proof → the reframe → what it means for the reader → one honest line about why we care.

## 6. Post types and shapes

- **Story** (~1,000–1,300 chars): start mid-action → what happened (real numbers) → the turn → the lesson in one line.
- **Lesson / framework** (save-worthy, ~1,300–1,800): name it only if it's genuinely repeatable; 3–6 uneven steps,
  1–2 lines each; end with a question or "save this for your next event".
- **Contrarian take** (~600–1,000): bold stance in line 1, 2–3 pieces of evidence, no softening.
- **Newsjack** (~800–1,300): §5.
- **Event / result** (bottom funnel): only confirmed facts; what the guest gets, not what we do.
- **Personal** (5–8 lines): feeling over data, still ends in the lane.

## 7. Writing rules that separate "real founder" from "AI wrote this"

- Simple words, first person, sentences under ~20 words, fragments fine. One thought per line, blank lines between.
- Take a side. No "on one hand / on the other hand".
- Specific beats vague: numbers, names (when allowed), times, places. No invented ones.
- Uneven lists: not always 3 or 5 items, not all the same length. No "three pillars" framing.
- No intro paragraph, no summary at the end, no tacked-on moral, no upbeat bow. End on the punchline or an honest
  line.
- No em/en dashes as separators (voice.md), no decorative emojis (max 1–2 functional), no bold headers in posts.
- **Banned words**: leverage, navigate, unlock, empower, delve, tapestry, synergy, game-changer, thought leadership,
  deep dive, streamline, foster, robust, holistic, nuanced, landscape, paradigm, resonate, comprehensive,
  facilitate, optimize, catalyst.
- **Banned phrases**: In today's fast-paced world · It's worth noting · I'm excited to announce · Let me share ·
  Here's the thing · The key takeaway · Not only… but also · At the end of the day · A testament to · It goes
  without saying · Serves as a reminder · In an era where · It's important to remember · Furthermore / Moreover /
  Additionally / That being said / With that in mind · Imagine… / Picture this… · In conclusion / To sum up · I'd
  love to hear your thoughts · The journey continues.
- No fake quotes around concepts nobody said.

## 8. Quality gate (every draft, before it is shown)

Score each line yes/no; ship only drafts with ≥ 14/16, and say the score.
1. Hook stops the scroll and signals the topic in ≤ 2 lines.
2. One topic only; fits a pillar and a funnel stage.
3. Written for the buyer (event/field/GTM people), in their words.
4. At least one concrete, true specific (number, scene, detail).
5. Save-worthy element or a real stance.
6. Every fact traceable to a source; nothing invented; disclosure rules respected (pavoot-today.md §7).
7. No banned words or phrases; no AI transitions.
8. No intro, no summary close, no moral bow.
9. Uneven rhythm: mixed line lengths, lists not 3/5 by default.
10. Sentences you'd say out loud (read-aloud test).
11. No dashes as separators, ≤ 2 functional emojis, no bold headers.
12. No external link in the body (first comment instead).
13. Length fits the type (§6).
14. Ending is the punchline, an honest line, or a real question.
15. Media plan is real (a real photo/screenshot named, or text-only); no generated images.
16. It required being Ana (or Gohar) to write it.
Last pass, every draft: read it word by word for grammar, missing or doubled words and broken sentences, and fix them
(a broken sentence fails the gate no matter the score).
