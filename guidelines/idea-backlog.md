## Saved 2026-09-06

- **We add a delay so it doesn't look automatic.** We approve event registrations by hand. Most of
  the time we can tell from the email address alone, in about a second, whether the person belongs
  in the room. But we wait before hitting approve, because an instant approval reads as a bot and a
  bot reads as "nobody actually looked at me." So the human does the work in one second and then
  sits on it. The post is that small confession, told straight: the speed was never the hard part,
  the judgment was, and we deliberately hide the speed to protect how it feels on the other end.
  The insight for anyone running events: response *timing* is a message, separate from the response
  itself, and almost nobody designs it on purpose.
  WHO IT REACHES: this one is genuinely marketer-shaped. Approval flows, waitlists and invite lists
  are field marketing / event organizer daily work, not founder-Twitter material. Good candidate for
  a post that finally lands outside the engineer-and-founder demographic. Consider company page or
  Ana, and tag an organizer if we use a real event.
  NEEDS: which event(s), roughly how many registrations we screened by hand, and the actual delay we
  use (minutes? hours? end of day?). Ask Ana before drafting, do not guess the number.
  WATCH OUT: two traps. (1) Do not let it read as "the AI event company approves things manually
  because the product can't." Frame it as a choice about the recipient's experience, not a gap.
  (2) Do not moralize at the end about what this says about us. State the behaviour, state what it
  taught us about timing, stop. Also adjacent to the AGI / "Ana & Gohar Intelligence" seed above,
  same manual-work territory, so pick one to run and space the other out.
